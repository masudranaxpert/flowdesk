# Daily Conversation — Everyday English Speaking

You might be able to read and write English — but when it's time to speak, your mind goes blank! This happens to everyone. The reason is that we learn "book English," but the way people actually talk is different.

In this chapter, we'll learn 100+ everyday sentences — exactly the kind you need when talking to friends, coworkers, or a shopkeeper. Each category is separate, so you can jump straight to what you need.

> [!tip] How to Read This Chapter
> Say each sentence out loud — just reading with your eyes won't help. The biggest mistake in learning English is only reading, never speaking. Start today, one sentence at a time, say it aloud.

---

## 1. Greetings and Small Talk

What do you say when you see someone at the start of the day? "Good morning, how are you?" — that's fine, but it sounds a bit old-fashioned. Nowadays people speak more casually. Let's take a look.

| Bengali | English | Note |
|---------|---------|------|
| What's the news? | What's up? | Most common with friends |
| How are you? | How are you? | Formal, works anywhere |
| How's everything? | How's it going? | Like "What's up" but a bit softer |
| Long time no see! | Long time no see! | Say this when meeting after a while |
| Where have you been? | Where have you been? | When you see someone after a long gap |
| Is everything okay? | Everything okay? | When someone is quiet or looks worried |
| What's happening? | What's going on? | When something is happening around you |
| What's new? | What's new? | When you bump into an old friend |
| Are you good? | You good? | Very informal, like asking a buddy |
| Doing okay | I'm doing okay / Not bad | The safest reply you can give |

> [!tip] How to reply to "What's up?"
> The easiest reply is — "Nothing much, you?" It means "Not much, what about you?" This is the most common response.

```
Arafat: Hey! What's up?
Sadia:  Nothing much. You?
Arafat: Just chilling. Want to grab a coffee?
Sadia:  Sure, let's go!
```

```
Rakib:  Long time no see! Where have you been?
Nila:   I was back home in the village.
Rakib:  Oh nice! How was it?
Nila:   Amazing! I explored a lot.
```

## 2. Asking Questions

Sometimes even very simple questions slip our mind. Let's look at them together.

| Bengali | English | Note |
|---------|---------|------|
| What are you doing? | What are you doing? | Most-used phrase on WhatsApp |
| Where are you going? | Where are you going? | When someone is rushing out |
| What are you thinking? | What are you thinking? | When someone is being quiet |
| How's it going? | How's it going? | An easy way to check in on someone |
| What happened? | What happened? | When you hear or notice something |
| Why is that? | Why is that? | To ask the reason for something |
| Who told you? | Who told you? | When you hear news or gossip |
| When are you going? | When are you going? | To find out someone's schedule |
| What for? | What for? | Informal version of "Why?" |
| And then? | And then? | To keep a story going |

```
Mim:   What are you doing?
Hasan: Nothing much, just watching YouTube.
Mim:   What are you watching?
Hasan: A coding tutorial.
```

> [!note] "What" vs "How"
> "What are you doing?" means "What activity are you doing?" — asking about the action. "How are you doing?" means "How are you feeling?" — asking about your state. Keep this difference in mind.

## 3. Expressing Feelings

Emotions, joy, sadness — everything needs to be expressed. These are especially important with friends.

| Bengali | English | Note |
|---------|---------|------|
| Not feeling well | I don't feel good | Works for both physical and mood |
| Really enjoying it | I'm really enjoying it | When you like something a lot |
| It's annoying | It's annoying / That's frustrating | When something keeps happening |
| I'm scared | I'm scared / That's scary | For movies, news, or situations |
| I'm surprised | I'm surprised / That's surprising | When something unexpected happens |
| I'm sorry | I'm sorry / I'm sad | "I'm sorry" = apology, "I'm sad" = feeling down |
| I'm happy | I'm happy / I'm glad | When you hear good news |
| I can't wait | I can't wait | To express excitement |
| I'm exhausted | I'm exhausted | At the end of the day or after work |
| I'm angry | I'm angry / I'm mad | In American English, "mad" = angry |

```
Tania: How's the movie?
Sajid: I'm really enjoying it! It's great.
Tania: Me too. Wait, this scene is scary!
Sajid: Yeah, that's scary!
```

> [!tip] "I'm sorry" vs "I'm sad"
> This confuses a lot of people. "I'm sorry" means "I apologize" — you say it when asking for forgiveness. "I'm sad" means you're genuinely feeling down. Remember — "sorry" can be used for both apologizing and showing sympathy.

## 4. Plans and Decisions

What to do, when to do it — everything needs a plan. These sentences are needed every single day.

| Bengali | English | Note |
|---------|---------|------|
| I won't go to university today | I won't go to university today | When you need a break |
| I'll do it tomorrow | I'll do it tomorrow | The classic procrastination line |
| Okay / Sure | Okay / Sure / Alright | The most common responses |
| Let's go | Let's go | For two people or a group |
| I'll check later | I'll see later / I'll check later | When you don't want to think now |
| Let me think | Let me think | When you need time |
| I've decided | I've decided | When the decision is made |
| I'll try | I'll try | Not promising, just trying |
| I'm not sure | I'm not sure | When there's doubt |
| What's the plan? | What's the plan? | When you need to decide something |

```
Imran: What's the plan tomorrow? Going out?
Faria: I'm not sure. What are you saying?
Imran: Let's go watch a movie!
Faria: Okay, let's! Which theater?
Imran: Let me think, we'll decide today.
```

> [!example] Common Pattern
> "I'll + verb" expresses the future — "I'll go," "I'll call," "I'll check." This pattern is used dozens of times every day.

## 5. Food and Hunger

Talking about food — there's no escaping it! It's the most common topic in people's lives.

| Bengali | English | Note |
|---------|---------|------|
| Have you eaten? | Have you eaten? | The Bengali equivalent of "How are you?" |
| I'm hungry | I'm hungry | The simplest thing to say |
| What will you eat? | What will you eat? / What do you want? | At a restaurant or at home |
| Want some water? | Want some water? | When a guest arrives |
| It tastes great | It tastes great / It's delicious | When you're impressed |
| This isn't tasty | This doesn't taste good | When food isn't good, said simply |
| I'm full | I'm full / I can't eat anymore | When your stomach is full |
| I'm starving | I'm starving | When you're very hungry |
| Want some tea? | Want some tea? | Offering tea to someone |
| Shall we order? | Shall we order? | At a restaurant |

```
Waiter: What would you like to order?
Rana:  One biryani, please.
Rina:  Beef curry for me.
Waiter: Anything to drink?
Rana:  Two Cokes.
```

> [!tip] The real meaning of "Starving"
> "I'm starving" literally means you're dying of hunger — but of course, it's not literal. It's a very common exaggeration. It's the same vibe as saying "I'm dying of hunger" in Bengali.

## 6. Time and Schedule

Time is life. When and where you need to go — these sentences are the most essential.

| Bengali | English | Note |
|---------|---------|------|
| What time will you go? | What time will you go? | To know the schedule |
| How long will it take? | How long will it take? | To estimate how long a task takes |
| I'm late | It's late / I'm running late | When you need to hurry |
| I need to go now | I need to go now / I gotta go | When it's urgent |
| Time's up | Time's up / It's time | When time is over |
| What time is the meeting? | What time is the meeting? | At the office or university |
| Give me five minutes | Give me five minutes | When you need a little time |
| I'll finish today | I'll finish it today | When there's a deadline |
| There's still time | There's still time | When there's no rush |
| On time | On time / Punctually | Exactly at the right time |

```
Sajid: What time is the class?
Nila:  Ten o'clock. But I'm running late!
Sajid: How long will it take?
Nila:  Another fifteen minutes.
Sajid: Okay, come quickly.
```

> [!note] "Gotta" is short for "have got to"
> "I gotta go" means "I have to go right now." It's informal but used every day. In formal settings, say "I need to go" or "I must go."

## 7. Work and Study

University, job, assignments — talking about all of it. These sentences are needed by every student and professional.

| Bengali | English | Note |
|---------|---------|------|
| Is work done? | Is work done? / Are you done? | To check progress |
| Have you studied? | Have you studied? | Most common during exam season |
| Very busy | I'm very busy / I'm swamped | When you're buried in work |
| I have work | I have work to do | When there's no free time |
| Day off | I'll take a day off today | When you need rest |
| Assignment pending | I have an assignment due | When the deadline is approaching |
| Presentation today | I have a presentation | When stage fright kicks in |
| In progress | It's in progress / I'm working on it | Happening, but not done yet |
| Submitted? | Have you submitted? | To check on a deadline |
| Need a break | I need a break | When you're tired |

```
Sumi:  Is the assignment done?
Rana:  No, I'm working on it. Need two more hours.
Sumi:  No rush, I'm not done either.
Rana:  Good. Let's do it together.
```

> [!tip] "Swamped" — a useful word
> "I'm swamped" means "I'm drowning in work" — extremely busy. It's a stronger word than "busy." Very useful in an office setting.

## 8. Phone and Chat

Talking on the phone and chatting are two different things. In chat, short sentences work; on the phone, full sentences are needed.

| Bengali | English | Note |
|---------|---------|------|
| I was calling you | I was calling you / I tried calling you | When they didn't pick up |
| I messaged you | I messaged you / I texted you | When there's no reply |
| You didn't reply | You didn't reply | A slight complaint |
| Call me back | Call me back | Before hanging up |
| I'll call you back | I'll call you back | When you're busy now |
| What are you up to? | What are you up to? | Very common in chat |
| You weren't online | You weren't online | When the reply is late |
| Phone was off | My phone was off / dead | When the battery died |
| Video call? | Let's video call | When you need to see each other |
| Hello? | Hello? | The first thing said when picking up |

```
[Phone rings]
Nila:   Hello?
Hasan:  Hey! I tried calling you earlier.
Nila:   Sorry, my phone was on silent.
Hasan:  No worries. What were you up to?
Nila:   Nothing much, just reading a book.
```

> [!note] "I tried calling" vs "I was calling"
> "I tried calling you" = I called but you didn't pick up. "I was calling you" = I was in the process of calling. The first one is more natural.

## 9. Asking for Help

Never feel shy about asking for help. Everyone helps each other. Let's learn how to ask nicely.

| Bengali | English | Note |
|---------|---------|------|
| Can you do a favor? | Can you do me a favor? | For small tasks |
| I need help | I need help / I need a hand | For bigger tasks |
| I don't understand | I don't understand / I don't get it | When confused |
| Say it again | Say it again / Could you repeat that? | When you didn't hear |
| Show me how | Can you show me how? | For practical things |
| What do you think? | What do you think? | To get an opinion |
| I can't help | I can't help with that | Politely declining |
| Bear with me | Bear with me | When you need time |
| Can you explain? | Can you explain? | When a concept isn't clear |
| Give me a second | Give me a second | When you need a brief moment |

```
Rima:  Can you do me a favor?
Arif:  Sure, what is it?
Rima:  Can you look at this assignment? I don't understand it.
Arif:  Let me see. Hmm, I don't get it either.
Rima:  Then let's ask the teacher.
```

> [!tip] Using "Favor" correctly
> "Can you do me a favor?" is the normal way to ask for a small task. For bigger tasks, "Can you help me with...?" is more natural. "Do a favor" is always for small things.

## 10. Refusal and Disagreement

Learning to say "no" is also a skill. Saying yes to everything makes life harder. Let's learn how to say no politely.

| Bengali | English | Note |
|---------|---------|------|
| I can't | I can't / I won't be able to | A direct no |
| I don't feel like it | I don't feel like it | When you're not in the mood |
| That's wrong | That's not right / That's wrong | Pointing out a mistake |
| I disagree | I disagree / I don't agree | A difference of opinion |
| No way | No way / Not a chance | A firm no |
| I don't think so | I don't think so | When there's doubt |
| Maybe later | Maybe later / I'll pass for now | Polite refusal |
| I'll pass | I'll pass | Very polite no |
| That's impossible | That's impossible | An absolute no |
| That won't work | That won't work | When a plan fails |

```
Tamim: Let's go to the party today!
Zara:  I don't feel like it. I'm tired.
Tamim: Come on! Everyone's going.
Zara:  No way, I'm not going.
Tamim: Okay, maybe next time.
```

> [!important] The etiquette of saying "No"
> Always say no gently. Instead of "No, I can't," saying "I don't think I can" or "Maybe next time" keeps relationships healthy. In Bengali culture, a direct no is considered rude — the same applies in English.

## Tips for Remembering

> [!tip] 5 Golden Rules
> 1. **Say it out loud** — just reading won't work
> 2. **Remember the context** — think about when to use each sentence
> 3. **Practice responses** — think about what the reply could be for each sentence
> 4. **Use in real conversations** — with friends, at work, wherever you can
> 5. **Don't be afraid of mistakes** — without mistakes, there's no learning

> [!example] A One-Day Practice Plan
> - Wake up in the morning and say "Good morning!" in front of the mirror
> - Send a friend a message in English on WhatsApp
> - At lunch, say "It tastes great!" out loud
> - In the afternoon, start a conversation with "What's up?"
> - At night, text "I'm tired, gotta sleep" before going to bed
