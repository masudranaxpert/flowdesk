# Phrasal Verbs — 50+ Everyday Verb Phrases

You know the word "get," and you know the word "up." But what does "get up" mean? Even though you know them separately, together the meaning changes completely! "Get up" means "to rise from sleep."

These kinds of phrases are called **phrasal verbs** — verb + preposition/adverb = a brand-new meaning. English has hundreds of such verb phrases. But in this chapter, we'll learn the most common ones — 50+ phrasal verbs organized under 18 main verbs.

> [!tip] Why Learn Phrasal Verbs?
> Native speakers can't speak without phrasal verbs. "I'll call you back" — here "call back" is a phrasal verb. You'll hear them in movies, TV series, and podcasts everywhere. Without knowing them, you'll miss a lot.

## Get — The Most Versatile Verb

"Get" creates the most phrasal verbs. Just this one verb has 7-8 of them!

| Phrasal Verb | Bengali Meaning | English Meaning | Example |
|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| get up | to wake up | to wake up and leave bed | I **get up** at 6 AM every day. |
| get along | to get along | to have a good relationship | I **get along** well with my boss. |
| get over | to recover | to recover from something | It took me a month to **get over** the flu. |
| get away | to escape | to escape / go on a break | Let's **get away** this weekend! |
| get back | to return | to return | When did you **get back** from Dhaka? |
| get in | to enter | to enter | We **got in** the car and left. |
| get off | to get off | to leave a vehicle | **Get off** at the next stop. |
| get rid of | to get rid of | to remove / throw away | I need to **get rid of** old clothes. |

> [!note] The fun of "Get"
> "Get" alone is used in over a hundred meanings. "I get it" (I understand), "I get angry" (I become angry), "Get out!" (Leave!) — understand the meaning from context.

```
Mom:   What time did you get up?
Son:   Seven. But I'm not feeling well today.
Mom:   What happened?
Son:   I think I have a fever. Maybe the flu.
Mom:   Then rest. You'll get over it in a couple of days.
```

| Phrasal Verb | Bengali Meaning | English Meaning | Example |
|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| look for | to search | to search | I'm **looking for** my keys. |
| look after | to take care of | to take care of | She **looks after** her grandmother. |
| look up | to look up | to search (in a book/online) | **Look up** the word in the dictionary. |
| look forward to | to look forward to | to be excited about | I **look forward to** the weekend! |
| look into | to investigate | to investigate | The police are **looking into** the case. |
| look out | watch out! | watch out / be careful | **Look out!** There's a car coming. |

## Memory Tricks

> [!tip] Techniques for Remembering
> 1. **Visualize** — when you say "get up," picture yourself waking up from bed
> 2. **Use in sentences** — don't just read a list, put them in your own sentences
> 3. **Remember opposite pairs** — put on / put off, turn on / turn off
> 4. **Match context** — when you hear them in movies or series, take note
> 5. **Study in groups** — learn all phrasal verbs of the same verb together

> [!note] Types of Phrasal Verbs
> There are two types of phrasal verbs:
> - **Separable** — the object can go in the middle: "turn it on" or "turn on the light"
> - **Inseparable** — cannot be separated: "look after her" (not "look her after")
> In most cases, a pronoun (it, them, him) goes in the middle.
